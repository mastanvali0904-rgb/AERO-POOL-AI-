AERO-POOL AI: Full Engineering Specification & Mathematical Models

The Core Problem: Indian Metropolitan Airport Last-Mile Cliff

Indian international airports (BLR, HYD, BOM, DEL) are situated 30–45 km away from primary city business hubs. Arriving travelers face a binary dilemma: an inconvenient ₹300 bus (slow, infrequent, distant drop-offs with heavy luggage) versus an exorbitant ₹1,300 solo cab (causes 10,000+ daily deadhead trips, severe curb congestion). Traditional carpooling failed because passengers booking before baggage claim desynchronize, and compact CNG sedans (Dzire/Etios) with 60L gas cylinders have only 180L boot space, triggering trunk overflow and curb cancellations.

1. Volumetric Trunk Constraint CV BOUNDED
V_total = ∑(i=1 to N) V_i ≤ 500 Liters

Ensures all baggage fits behind the 3rd row in allocated 6-seater MPVs/e-SUVs. Automatically rejects 180L CNG sedans whenever V_total > 180L to eliminate passenger-lap luggage hazards.

2. Radial Cluster Boundary CORRIDOR SLA
R_cluster = max(i,j) D(P_i, P_j) ≤ 4.0 km

Strictly bounds the drop-off locations within a 4 km radial envelope along the expressway stem, preventing zig-zag detours across distant city sectors.

3. Detour SLA Threshold MAX 12 MINS
ΔT = T_pool - T_direct < 12.0 mins

Guarantees that no passenger suffers more than 12 minutes of aggregate detour compared to a solo direct cab ride.

4. Post-Belt Batching Window EXIT-GATE SYNC
W_batch ≤ 6.0 mins (360 seconds)

Eliminates belt desynchronization by triggering cohort assembly only after passengers physically clear baggage claim turnstiles.
